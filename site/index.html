Live translation

Press Ctrl+Alt+L in a Teams or Zoom call, a video, or a room: what people say appears in a bar over any app, the words heard above their translation.

  • Translated while people speak

    Each line appears as it's said. Scroll back through the session, or copy all of it in both languages.

  • Read aloud, like an interpreter

    Gemini says the translation in its own voice, and the meeting goes quieter under it.

  • Both ways

    The meeting into English, and your own words into Japanese, marked "You".

  • The cost saver

    Audio goes to Google only while someone talks: silence costs nothing, and the bar shows what you saved.

  • Hidden from screen sharing

    The bar stays out of shares and recordings, unless you switch it on for colleagues to read.

Screen translator

Press Ctrl+Alt+S: a frame goes over any app, and the Japanese inside it is covered with English, where it was, in its own colours.

  • Internal tools, documents, web pages

    Menus, tables, buttons and paragraphs, each in place. It follows as you scroll.

  • The app still works underneath

    Clicks go through the frame. Press the eye to see the original for a moment.

  • Read on your laptop

    Windows' own text recognition reads the screen; only the new words go to Gemini to translate.

Translate any text

Select text in any app and press CtrlCC: the translation pops up, to copy or to put in its place. Or type it on Translate's Text tab, from any language into any other.

Meeting mode

Every meeting, written up.

Rflow finds your Teams, Zoom, Google Meet or Webex meeting. Press Start, or Ctrl+Alt+M: it's translated live, on screen or out loud, and recorded on your laptop. When it ends, Gemini writes the minutes.

  • Hear it in your language

    They speak Japanese; you read it in English on Rflow's bar. Turn on Speak the translation and you hear it in English too, with the call turned down.

  • Finds the meeting by itself

    Start when it begins; it stops by itself when the meeting window closes.

  • Recorded on this laptop

    You and the others as separate sound files, in a folder of the meeting's own.

  • The minutes, written for you

    Who said what, a summary, the decisions, and the action items with owners and dates. The slides shown help too.

  • In person, too

    A meeting in a room: your microphone hears everyone, with the same captions and minutes.

FAQ

Questions

Which languages?

Live translation and Translate: most of the world's major languages, into and out of each other. The Screen translator reads Japanese into English. Voice typing: English with Parakeet on your laptop, and 83 languages, Japanese and Hindi among them, with Gemini Transcribe (the Multilingual setup) or Whisper.

What does it cost?

Rflow itself is free. What uses Gemini is paid to Google with your own key, at its list prices: about $1 a month for 20 minutes of voice typing a day, $2.20 an hour of live translation (less with the cost saver), and about 26¢ for an hour's minutes. Rflow's Home shows this month's spending, by feature. Voice typing on your PC alone costs nothing.

Do I need a Google key?

For live translation, Meeting mode's minutes, Translate and the Screen translator, yes: a Gemini API key, which you can create for free in Google AI Studio and paste into Rflow once. Voice typing works without one.

What is sent, and where?

Voice typing with Parakeet or Whisper stays on your laptop. Live translation sends what it listens to (other people's voices too) to Google Gemini, and says so before it starts. Meeting mode records on your laptop; to write the minutes, it sends the recording to Gemini. Translate and the Screen translator send only the text. Nothing goes to Rflow: there's no Rflow server.

Is there a phone app?

Rflow for Android is coming, starting with live translation and Meeting mode for meetings in the room; voice typing, translating text and the Screen translator follow. It's open source too; follow it on GitHub. For a Teams or Zoom call on the phone itself, use Rflow for Windows: Android doesn't let apps hear the sound of a call.

Is it really open source?

Yes, under the MIT License. The code, the issues and every change are on GitHub; bug reports, ideas and pull requests are welcome.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

The installer isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

What does it need?

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), a microphone and about 1 GB of disk space: about 300 MB for Rflow and 660 MB for Parakeet. No GPU, no admin rights, no account.

How do updates work?

Rflow checks for a new version when it starts and every few hours: click Update now in its window, or Check for updates at its top. The download is checked against its published checksum before it's installed, and Rflow restarts itself.

Nothing is typed in one particular app

That app probably runs as administrator; Windows doesn't let normal programs type into those.
